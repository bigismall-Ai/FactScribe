摘要

Scientific papers written with language models carry numbers no reader can trace, citations no file supports, and prose that looks fluent and reads the same every time. We present FactScribe, a mechanical gate built on a contract rather than a model. Every number in a manuscript is bound to a registered fact; every fact points at a run record with content hashes; every citation must resolve to the bibliography; prose is scanned for countable regularities and reported, never silently rewritten. The contract fixes the input grammar, the fact schema, the code table, and the binding rule before any document is judged, because our early failures were contract failures, not detector failures. In nine sealed blind sets, first-run detection rose from 0/12✓ to 11/12✓; across the three sets run after the freeze the gate caught 30/36✓ planted defects with 0/36✓ false alarms on valid projects. A style gate from the same toolchain marks the boundary: it improved a raw draft, where reviewers preferred the guided version on 9/10✓ pairs, and degraded polished prose, where the rewrite won only 3/24✓ of comparisons. The blind protocol is weak—author, implementer, and scorer shared one machine—and the trust root is a human signature, so the gate certifies that the account, the evidence, and the prose agree, not that the claims are true.

一、为什么现在还不能信 AI 写的论文Introduction

A manuscript drafted with a language model fails in ways that a reader cannot check from the page. Two of those failures are fabrications. A number can be plausible and wrong, a percentage that no run produced, carried through a paragraph because it resembles the numbers around it. A citation can be well formed and nonexistent, with author, year, venue, and a title assembled from the vocabulary of the field. Neither announces itself. Checking either one means leaving the sentence and opening a source, and a reviewer with a deadline rarely does.

Other failures are quieter and more common in our own pipeline. A typesetting comment survives into the compiled PDF and prints as body text. An internal code name appears in a sentence meant for readers. A pasted debug transcript takes the place of a methods paragraph, because it was the nearest artefact when the section was written. A disclaimer is scattered into sentence-level hedges instead of stated once, so that no single sentence admits what the paper as a whole cannot support. A quantitative sentence carries no marker that would let a reader find the number's origin. None of these is a lie, and all of them are unreviewable for the same reason: the page shows text, and the text does not say where it came from.

The standard response is a stronger prompt: instruct the model to invent no numbers, to cite only real work, to avoid placeholders. We do not believe this can work, for a structural reason. A prompt is a request to the generator, and the generator's output is the only evidence that the request was honoured. Nothing in the loop can separate a sentence that is true from a sentence that is fluent, because the same process produces both and both read well. The check has to run after generation, on artefacts that can be inspected without trusting the generator: the rendered text, the fact ledger, and the evidence chain behind each fact.

This paper describes FactScribe, a mechanical gate built on that premise. The gate reads the TeX closure of a project, extracts every numeral, and requires each numeral to be covered by a registered fact in its own sentence. Each fact must name a source file, a content hash, and a receipt, and a break anywhere in that chain is a hard failure. The compiled PDF is then re-read and compared with the ledger in both directions, so that a digit present in the paper but absent from the ledger, or absent from the paper but present in the ledger, is reported. Judgement of meaning is out of scope. The gate can say that a number is unregistered. It cannot say that a registered number is the right measurement.

The design choice that mattered most was not a rule but a freeze. Our early sealed tests failed for reasons that had little to do with detection. The first set scored 0/12✓. By the seventh it reached 11/12✓, and the gain did not come from adding rules. Each early failure traced to a surface the specification had left open: the shape of the project file, the shape of the fact ledger, and the code table that names findings. Independent authors wrote incompatible project files for the same task, and the implementation accepted one form. Once those surfaces were written down and frozen, the remaining work was implementation. Across the three sets that stayed valid to the freeze, the gate reached 30/36✓ hits with 0/36✓ false positives, against a bar of 10/12✓ registered before the runs, over 9✓ sealed sets.

A second lesson concerns criteria that are not mechanical. A rule can fire correctly and still make the text worse. Our style gate counts 8✓ regularities without a model in the loop: stock connectives, sentence-length variation, empty adjectives, three-item lists, paragraph-ending templates, stacked hedges, repeated openings, and long sentences without internal structure. On a raw draft, 6✓ of the indicators fired, and following the rewrite list helped, with the reviewers preferring the guided version on 9/10✓ and 9/10✓ pairs. On a polished manuscript the same family of rules lost, with the rewrite winning 4/17✓ and 5/17✓ pairs. The mechanical readings improved in both settings. Only the readers changed their verdict. A claim that a rewrite improves prose is a quality claim, and a quality claim needs a blind comparison, not a smaller count of findings.

We make three contributions. First, a contract-first gate. We freeze the three surfaces a mechanical check depends on, the code table, the project configuration, and the fact ledger schema, and we bind rendered numerals to ledger entries at sentence granularity, with a hash-anchored evidence chain and a bidirectional PDF recheck. The gate reports. It never edits the ledger or the manuscript.

Second, a sealed protocol for testing the gate itself. Thresholds are registered before a set is run, sets are hashed when completed, and every first-run score is kept, including the ones that failed. The protocol is weak in a way we state plainly: the author of the sets and the author of the implementation share a machine.

Third, one negative result with a usable rule. Style rewriting is a draft-stage intervention. On polished text the same rules that help a draft lose to the untouched manuscript, so the tool reports and does not rewrite once a text is polished. We also publish the gaps we did not close.

The rest of the paper follows the artefacts. The design section describes the layers and the tradeoffs behind them. The case study reports the style gate and its blind comparisons. The blind-test section reports the sealed sets for the fact gate, and the limitations section states what the tool cannot do. It cannot detect a forger who controls both the ledger and the run records, it does not read images, and its blind protocol is disciplined rather than isolated.

二、别人做过什么，我们差在哪Related Work

Our work sits where three lines meet: systems that generate scientific text, infrastructure that records where a claim came from, and protocols that test a quality judgement without letting the judge see the answer.

Generation systems.

A growing line of work builds pipelines that draft a paper end to end （[缺作者], 2026）（[缺作者], 2026）（[缺作者], 2025）. A planner turns a topic or a result set into a section structure, a retrieval step collects sources, a writer produces prose section by section, and a critic or reviewer model scores the draft. These systems are evaluated on fluency, coverage, and the verdict of another model, and their object of optimisation is the manuscript as a whole. Where this paper differs is the unit of concern. We do not try to produce a better draft and we do not score one. We take a draft as given and ask, numeral by numeral, whether each quantitative claim can be discharged against a registered source. A pipeline that writes well and a gate that checks well are complementary, and the second is what a reader needs before trusting the first.

Prose as a statistical object.

A second line measures model influence on prose at the population level. Excess vocabulary in biomedical abstracts tracks model adoption （Kobak 等, 2025）, and phrase-level mapping shows a rising share of model-typical wording in computational papers （Liang 等, 2024）. Reader studies show that assisted writing shifts what people take away from a text （Jakesch 等, 2023）, and that fluency does not settle the question of quality （Chakrabarty 等, 2024）. These results motivate counting regularities instead of guessing intent, which is the choice our style gate makes. Where this paper differs is the target of the count. We do not estimate whether a passage was model-written. We count surface regularities only to advise a writer, and we treat the count as advisory, because in our own experiments the count and the reader disagreed.

Provenance and reproducibility.

Work on reproducibility ties records to artefacts: dataset versions, code commits, container images, execution logs, and, in the stronger proposals, a verifiable link from a published result back to the run that produced it （[缺作者], 2026）（[缺作者], 2026）. Artifact evaluation and provenance models share one assumption, that the recorded object is a computation and the reader can rerun it. Where this paper differs is the granularity of the record and the point of enforcement. We attach provenance to numerals in the prose, at the sentence, and make a broken chain a failing exit code rather than a metadata warning. A study can be fully reproducible and still print a number that no run produced. The gate is aimed at that gap, where the computation is sound and the sentence is not.

Blind evaluation.

Blind comparison is standard in human evaluation: two versions, random order, a hidden key, and one question per pair. Pre-registration fixes the threshold before the data arrive （Nosek 等, 2018）, which is the only defence against moving the bar after a disappointing run. Where this paper differs is the object being tested. We apply the protocol to our own quality claims, including one we expected to hold and did not. The style gate improved every indicator it reports and lost the blind comparison, and we report both readings side by side.

Position.

The three lines converge on one requirement, an artefact that a third party can audit without rerunning the generator. That is what we try to build, and the rest of the paper is an account of what it costs.

三、先把契约写死，再让机器来判Contract-first design

The gate is small enough to read in an afternoon, by requirement rather than accident. Three layers carry the design: a ledger that says which numbers are registered, a gate that says which numbers the text claims, and a contract that says what a finding means.

Three Read-Only Inputs

The gate consumes a TeX closure, a fact ledger, and the run records the ledger points to, and produces findings with a code, a severity, and an exit status. It never writes to its inputs. If it could edit the ledger, a failing number could pass by editing the number rather than the evidence, and the audit would certify nothing.

The Fact Ledger

A ledger entry is one assertion with one number: an identifier, a claim, a value, an optional display form, a source path, a content hash, a locator, a confidence, and derivation fields for a computed entry. The ledger is the only place a number can originate. A numeral that is not in the ledger is not unverified but unregistered, and the gate has nothing to compare it to. The narrowing is deliberate: the gate checks registration, not truth.

Two details are load-bearing. The display form must be strictly equivalent to the value after canonicalisation, with no rounding tolerance, or a rounded restatement would pass as the original. And every entry carries an evidence chain of three links: a source under the run tree, a hash that matches the file on disk, and a receipt in the same directory listing the entry. A break at any link is red.

The ledger is append-only. A correction is a new entry naming the one it supersedes, and the hash anchors bytes rather than trust: it shows the file has not changed since the entry was written.

The Gate

Binding is per sentence, not per paragraph. The scanner extracts every numeral from the closure after masking comments, arguments that cannot render digits, and verbatim regions, and a marker closes at its own sentence boundary. Four outcomes follow. A numeral covered by a fact in its sentence, inside that fact's value set, is clean. A numeral inside a registered value set but with no marker is yellow: sloppy rather than invented. A numeral behind a marker but outside the value set is red, and so is a numeral that matches no value at all. An unknown identifier is red, a non-quantitative fact cannot back a numeral, and several facts in one sentence are attributed in token order.

The sentence is the smallest checkable unit: paragraph binding would let one marker cover a dozen numbers, and line binding would break on any wrapped sentence.

After the source layer passes, the compiled PDF is read back and counted by token class: numerals, fact markers, citations, cross-references. Fewer occurrences than the ledger is red, because text was dropped or an image replaced it; more is also red unless a whitelist names each extra token with its own source. A page with no text layer, or one dominated by a raster image, is red; the gate runs no OCR, so a quantity that exists only as pixels is refused at the source. A release then signs the report, the rules, the inputs, and the PDF hash with a key kept outside the project, since a key inside it travels with whatever it is meant to vouch for.

The Contract

Three surfaces are frozen with the specification: the code table, the project configuration, and the ledger schema. The code table assigns each finding one code, one severity, and one exit status, and it is closed, so an unregistered code in a report is itself a defect. The configuration has one canonical form, a closed alias list, and one required field, the closure entry point; unknown fields are ignored but reported, so the format can grow without silently changing a verdict. The schema fixes the container, the identifier alphabet, required fields, and the display rule.

Freezing is not documentation. It removes degrees of freedom that the implementation would otherwise fill by guessing. The failures before our freeze were contract failures: independent authors wrote incompatible project files, and a ledger without an identifier was read as a different format. The current rule set is covered by 256✓ test cases, up from 247✓ before a parser fix changed the measurement base.

Why No Model in the Judging Path

A verdict must be a function of bytes: the same tree gives the same exit code on any machine at any time. A model judge makes the verdict a function of weights and sampling, and the audit then depends on a service the reader may not have. Its rationale is also the least checkable text in the system. A model may still draft ledger entries, but each must carry a source, a hash, and a confidence, and the gate only checks the registration. The limit is accepted: the gate cannot tell a true registered fact from a false one, and its trust root is a human signature.

Why Every Applicable Code

A single-code report forces the implementation to rank rules, and rank changes between versions, so two runs can show different codes for the same defect. Reporting the full set makes findings a property of the passage rather than the traversal. When a sentence moved into an appendix breaks two rules, the two codes describe different repairs.

Why Three Levels

The level selects which layers run, never which reds are downgraded. L0 and L1 run the source layer; L2 adds the PDF recheck and the stamp. A clean run below L2 is unverified, so a draft cannot borrow the authority of a release. The source layer runs on every edit, while the PDF layer needs a build and a signature. The strict flag turns a yellow finding into a failing exit, so a warning cannot be ignored at release.

Report, Do Not Rewrite

The same split decides the style layer. Its 8✓ indicators are counted on the rendered text, and the gate returns a rewrite list for a draft or a report for a polished text. A raw draft of one section fired 6✓ indicators and received the list. The threshold lives in the configuration, not in a model's discretion.

Architecture

Read together, the layers answer three questions: what a number must be to be admissible, what the sentence claims, and what the disagreement means. Separating them is what makes two runs comparable.

四、文风闸：帮倒忙的那一次The Style Gate on Polished Prose

Motivation

Scientific prose written with a language model carries regularities that a reader can feel before naming them. Corpora show a burst of excess vocabulary after model adoption （Kobak 等, 2025）, a rising share of model-typical phrasing （Liang 等, 2024）, measurable shifts in what readers take away from assisted text （Jakesch 等, 2023）, and a gap between model fluency and human judgement of creative writing （Chakrabarty 等, 2024）. A gate cannot settle authorship from a paragraph. The tractable question is narrower: which regularities can be counted without a model in the loop, and what happens when a writer removes them?

We count 8✓ core indicators, and 9✓ when the rhythm indicator is included. A raw model draft of one evaluation section, written from the same fact ledger used throughout this paper, fired 6✓ of those indicators and produced 20✓ findings. The failure developed below is that clearing the surface does not always improve the text.

Indicators, Bidirectional Rules, and Stage

Each indicator is a ratio over scanned prose, not a judgement about meaning: stock connectives per thousand words, the coefficient of variation of sentence length, empty adjectives, three-item lists, paragraph-ending templates, stacked hedges, repeated paragraph openings, and sentences that are long without internal structure. The rhythm indicator finds a run of consecutive sentences in the same narrow length band inside one paragraph. Every rule lives in a configuration file and is recomputable.

Two choices matter. Rules are bidirectional where evidence supports it: a very low sentence-length CV is reported as too uniform, but a very high CV is not reported at all, because scatter is not a model signature. The tool is also stage-aware. Three triggers✓ or a CV below the lower bound mark a text as a draft and produce a rewrite list; otherwise the text is polished and the tool returns a report-only message. The healthy band is 0.45–0.80✓. The first version lacked this distinction, which explains much of what follows.

The first rule set treated a long sentence as a defect. It flagged 4✓ English sentences and 3✓ Chinese sentences as extreme, and the rewrite split each at a colon, dash, or clause boundary. The second rule set retired that rule for sentences with internal structure and added the rhythm indicator. It flagged 13✓ English plateaus and 12✓ Chinese ones, and the rewrite inserted a short sentence or merged two adjacent sentences at those points.

Three Comparisons and a Blind Protocol

We ran three comparisons. The first two take the published manuscript, a polished artefact, and rewrite only the sentences the gate marked. The third takes a raw draft of one section and asks the model to follow the gate's list. In every comparison two reviewers saw pairs in random A/B order without knowing which side was rewritten, and answered one question: which version reads more like a human expert wrote it? The first comparison used 17✓ pairs, covering every changed paragraph without sampling. The second used 24✓ pairs under the same rule, with a paragraph set that changed because the rule changed. The third used 10✓ pairs, one per paragraph of the drafted section. Reviewers saw neither the key nor each other's choices. The protocol is blind but weak: reviewers are language models, pairs are few, and everything ran on one machine.

Gate Wins on Drafts, Loses on Polished Text

On polished text both reviewers preferred the original. In the first round the rewrite won 4 of 17✓ and 5 of 17✓ pairs, at rates of 0.235✓ and 0.294✓; the reviewers agreed on 14/17✓ pairs. The second round did not improve: the rewrite won 5 of 24✓ and 3 of 24✓ pairs, at rates of 0.208✓ and 0.125✓. One reviewer chose the original on 21 of 24✓ pairs, and the two agreed on 20/24✓ pairs. Winning at most five comparisons is not a marginal loss; it is a consistent preference for the untouched text.

On a raw draft the same gate helped. Both reviewers chose the guided version on 9/10✓ and 9/10✓ pairs, and their choices agreed on 10/10✓ pairs. A first draft is formulaic; the gate names the formula; following the list improves the prose.

The mechanical readings moved in the same direction in both settings, which makes the contrast instructive. On the raw draft the sentence-length CV rose from 0.373✓ to 0.473✓, and findings fell from 20✓ to 0✓. On the polished manuscript the second rule set cleared the 13✓ English plateaus and the 12✓ Chinese ones, and moved CV to 0.550✓ and 0.556✓. By every number the tool reports, the polish improved; by the only number readers report, it got worse.

The first round contains a sharper version of the contradiction. In the Chinese evaluation section, splitting ten long sentences pushed the section-level CV from 0.475 down to 0.358✓, below the gate's own uniformity floor. The instruction said to break long sentences; following it made the section more uniform and tripped a different rule. The lesson is not that one threshold was wrong. It is that sentence-level instructions compose into a distribution the instruction did not model.

What Did Not Move

The rewriting never touched a fact. The first round kept 234✓ fact bindings before and after the edit, with zero red findings. The multiset of numbers, fact markers, citations, and cross-references stayed identical: 754 / 170 / 12 / 19✓ tokens in English and 751 / 171 / 12 / 19✓ in Chinese. The third round carried 66✓ bound numbers on both sides, with the same 49 / 29 / 70✓ fact sites, unique identifiers, and numeric tokens. A style gate that changed a digit would be a fact bug wearing a style costume; this one did not.

Fixing a parser bug later changed the ruler rather than the manuscript. The bug treated an escaped dollar sign as the start of mathematics and swallowed the rest of the file. After the fix the English manuscript measured 6390→6924✓ words, 368→397✓ sentences, and 90→96✓ paragraphs; the Chinese manuscript measured 10059→10893✓ characters. Tests grew from 247✓ to 256✓ cases, and the corrected CVs were 0.529✓ and 0.538✓. Earlier style readings were low by the swallowed share; the corrected values still sit inside the healthy band.

The same probe exposed a second limit. After we removed the parser blind spot and measured the parts of each draft the gate had never seen, the naive draft rose to 24✓ findings while the guided draft rose to 2✓ findings, and the guided CV fell to 0.417✓, below the healthy floor. The gap survives the correction, but the guided draft is clean only where the ruler reached.

For context, the fact gate in the same toolchain was tested separately, and its trajectory is the positive result this paper can defend. First-run hits rose from 0/12✓ to 11/12✓. The three sets that remained valid produced 30/36✓ hits with 0/36✓ false positives. The last two sets before the freeze hit 10/12✓ and 9/12✓. Nine✓ sets were run against a pre-registered bar of at least 10/12✓. That result is not a verdict on the style gate.

Why Polished Prose Is the Wrong Target

A long sentence in a polished manuscript usually carries structure: a colon introducing a list, a dash holding an aside, a clause that delays the main verb for emphasis. The first rule counted length and missed structure, so it flagged sentences an expert had built deliberately. The second rule recognised structure and stopped flagging them, but its rhythm rule then asked for another local edit: break the plateau. Those plateaus were often deliberate too, a run of parallel clauses holding a comparison together.

A published paragraph has also been tuned against a reader. Its lengths, openings, and connectives are correlated with the argument at that point. Changing one length under a local rule perturbs that correlation, and the reader notices the seam even when every sentence remains grammatical. The gate measures marginals; the reader experiences sequence. Only 1/17✓ paragraphs from the first round were still flagged under the second rule set, so the negative result is not one stubborn paragraph that both rules disliked. Two rule sets aimed at different regularities produced the same verdict on the same kind of text.

A Draft-Stage Intervention

The rule we keep is narrow and testable. Style rewriting is a draft-stage intervention. On a raw draft it removed 6✓ triggered indicators and produced a version both reviewers preferred on 9/10✓ pairs. On a polished manuscript the same family of rules lost as many as 21 of 24✓ comparisons, and the second rule set lost again after the first was retired. The tool should report, not rewrite, once a text is polished. That is now the default: the stage check returns a report-only message, and the rewrite list is available only for drafts.

Limitations

The reviewers are language models, not human experts, so every preference here is a model reading like an editor. The samples are small: the smallest blind set had 10✓ pairs and the largest 24✓. One model wrote and rewrote every draft. The third round's per-pair record was not preserved as a file, and its 9/10✓ figure rests on a summary rather than a recomputable table; the ledger marks that fact as medium confidence. All runs shared one machine and one provider key. Finally, the gate reads text, not argument: it can report that a paragraph looks templated and cannot report that the paragraph is wrong.

五、盲测：九套卷子画出的那条曲线Blind Tests

Why the Gate Was Tested Blind

A checker tuned on the documents it is graded on measures its author, not its rules. We separated the two roles. One seat read only the specification and wrote the test projects; another implemented the checker and did not see the tests until the first run. Each set contains hostile projects, each built around one defect, and benign projects that look similar but are correct. The set was hashed as a tree, the hash was recorded outside the repository, and the first run was the recorded run: no repair was allowed between seeing a score and writing it down. The pass rule was registered in advance. A set passes when at least 10/12✓ hostile projects are caught and no benign project is flagged. A catch counts only when the reported code is the one the project was built to trigger.

Nine Sets, One Curve

We ran nine sealed sets✓. Table 表 1·九套盲测 lists them in order, with the contract state at the time. Letters replace the set numbers used in the internal log.

The first set caught nothing: every project exited with a usage error before any check, because the implementation expected an earlier layout. Two further sets also caught nothing: the author wrote configuration in forms the specification had never fixed, and in another set the ledger entries carried no identifier, so the checker could not tell which fact authorised which number. Two more sets caught some but stayed under the bar. Both were scored against a code table that the specification mentioned but did not contain; one phenomenon could yield two codes, and a handful of sites were coded by guess. Folding the table into the specification and allowing multiple codes moved the first run to partial hits.

The seventh set was the first to clear the bar on its first run, at 11/12✓. The eighth caught 10/12✓ and the ninth 9/12✓. Across the three frozen sets the gate caught 30/36✓ hostile projects while flagging 0/36✓ benign ones.

The Curve Is Made of Contracts

Read as a sequence of scores, the curve looks like a detector slowly improving. Read as a sequence of changes, it is something else: no failing set was repaired by adding a rule. Each was repaired by writing down a contract both sides could read—the shape of a project configuration, the schema of a fact, the table of error codes. The detectors could already find the defects; they and the author disagreed about what a legal input looked like.

The configuration case is the clearest. The first and third sets came from an author working with a prose description; he produced three reasonable spellings of one declaration, and the implementation accepted one. The checker was not wrong: the specification had described an intention, and the author had to guess its file form.

The schema case repeats it one level down. Ledger entries in the fourth set were valid facts in every human sense and had no identifier, so no record could say which entry backed a cited number. The checker refused, correctly.

The code table is the subtlest. The specification said that a report cites codes and pointed at a table that lived elsewhere. Author and implementer built different tables, so a correct finding could be scored as the wrong code. Once the table was inside the specification and a site could carry more than one code, the scores moved without the detection logic changing.

This is the design claim in miniature: a gate is a contract with a judge attached, not a bag of heuristics. Most failures were places where the specification had been silent.

Red Team as a Separate Seat

Blind tests measure what the specification anticipated, not what it failed to imagine. For that we ran an adversarial review before the sets were frozen.

An external model, given only the specification, played attacker in its own session through a separate client; an internal red team read the implementation and attacked the code directly. The external seat filed sixty-one attacks over three rounds, and the internal team added seventeen in the first. Each attack was paired with a hostile fixture and a benign twin, so a proposed fix was scored both for catching the attack and for leaving correct documents alone.

The seats found different classes of defect, which argues for keeping both. The external seat, lacking the implementer's mental model, attacked the specification as a document: definitions readable two ways, guarantees naming a check without saying when it runs. The internal seat attacked the code: a rounding allowance that let a wrong ratio pass, an escape hatch that could hide a whole sentence, a debug macro that printed a placeholder into the PDF.

Most attacks were adopted outright, several partially, and one was rejected as incompatible with the read-only ledger; rejections are recorded with reasons.

What the Numbers Do and Do Not Say

The defensible result is narrow. On the three sets that were sealed and unread, the gate caught 30/36✓ planted defects and raised no false alarm on any of the 0/36✓ clean projects; two of the three cleared the pre-registered bar. This is evidence that the contract is executable: a specification, a schema, and a code table let an independent author write tests the implementation could pass unseen. It is not an estimate of field accuracy: the sets are small and the defect families are the ones the specification names. The ninth set proves the point: it missed a derived value whose expression disagreed with its value, a file included through a computed name, and a mismatch reported under a generic code.

Weak Blindness

The protocol is blind by discipline, not by permission. Test author, implementer, and scorer ran on one machine under one account. We can say the author was told to read only the specification and the tree hash was recorded before the run; we cannot prove no one looked. A determined implementer could read the sealed set and still report a clean run.

For the same reason the early scores are diagnostic only: they were produced while the contract was still moving, so a repair and a rerun are hard to separate from a lucky set. Only the last three sets carry the weight we claim. The stop line was part of the protocol: after the ninth set the gate would be frozen whatever the result, and any remaining defect published as a known gap rather than patched against the test.

六、局限：这套办法做不到什么Limitations

The Trust Root Is a Signature

The gate proves consistency, not occurrence. A fact points at a run record, the run record carries hashes of the request and the response, and the checker verifies that the number in the manuscript matches the value in the ledger and that the entry matches the record. If one agent writes the run record and the fact together, the chain closes on itself. Nothing in the data separates a real call from a fabricated pair of files.

What the gate adds is that the forgery must be complete and stable: the record, the fact, and the prose have to agree, and any later edit to a source breaks a hash. Whether a human approved the result is guaranteed only by a signature over the content. A signing key the agent can reach is a file location, not an identity, and if the agent can invoke the signing interface the tool cannot tell a human signature from a delegated one. This is a boundary of the design, not a bug waiting for a patch.

Weak Isolation

Test author and implementer shared a machine and an account, as Section 第五节·盲测 states. The separation was procedural: an instruction and a sealed hash, not a permission boundary. A stronger test would place the author outside the repository and the scorer outside the machine, with the key held by a person who does not write the paper. Until then every blind result in this paper should be read as evidence about the contract, not as an independent audit of the implementation.

Known Gaps

Three defects survived the freeze and are published in the release notes rather than patched. A derived fact may carry an expression that disagrees with its own value and still pass, so a computed number can be self-consistent and wrong. A file included through a computed name escapes the closure, so the scan can be complete over the files it sees and silent about one it does not. A genuine binding mismatch is reported under the generic unmatched-number code, which makes triage harder without changing the verdict. Each is a known hole in a named family, and each was left open on purpose: the stop line was registered before the last sets ran, and reopening the code after seeing a score is the practice the protocol exists to prevent.

Where the Mechanical View Fails

The style gate is the clearest limit of the whole approach. On polished manuscripts the rewrite was preferred on only 5/24✓ and 3/24✓ pairs; on a raw draft the guided version won 9/10✓ and 9/10✓. Every mechanical indicator improved in both settings, and the reader's verdict moved the other way. A count of surface features cannot see that a long sentence was doing work, and a rule that lowers one local number can raise the uniformity of the section around it.

Two further caveats apply to those readings. The reviewers were language models, not editors, so every preference is a model preference, and the samples are small. The gate can report that a paragraph looks templated; it cannot report that the argument is wrong. A checker that reads form should not be trusted with meaning, and a paper that passes every mechanical check is still a paper someone has to read. A clean report describes a manuscript and not the change that produced it: what a rewrite must not alter can be checked, and whether it improved the prose cannot.

七、结论Conclusion

A mechanical gate is reproducible only when its contract precedes its judge. The blind record in this paper—0/12✓ on the first set, 11/12✓ on the seventh, and 30/36✓ caught with 0/36✓ false alarms across the frozen sets—came from freezing the input grammar, the fact schema, and the code table. It did not come from adding detectors.

An adversary outside the implementation turns a specification into something executable. The attacks that mattered were about what the document left undefined, a class the internal team alone could not find.

Style rules are stage-specific. They help a raw draft, where both reviewers preferred the guided version on 9/10✓ pairs, and they hurt polished prose, where the rewrite won only 3/24✓ and 5/24✓ pairs. Once a text is polished, the tool should report and not rewrite.

The recommendation follows from the first two claims: freeze the contract, seal the tests, and register the pass bar before the first run. Until the trust root is a signature the author cannot reach, and the test author sits outside the implementer's machine, a green report means that the account, the evidence, and the prose agree. It does not mean the paper is true.
